Those rows are now **authorized V4b operations**. Standing V4 ops stay standing. A selector on a standing writer, or a scoped writer with no selector, **rejects, no write**.

#### Clocks (V4b-P2) — locked

Three clocks. Do not collapse them into “is this row’s date equal to today when the job runs.”

| Clock | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Occurrence** | Start day in the **schedule timezone** owns the window. An explicit overnight window (Monday 22:00–Tuesday 02:00) is one occurrence. Equal start/end is neither zero nor 24h. |
| **Lifetime** | When the declaration is in force. Outside it, the underlying arrangement resumes. The last selected occurrence date is not this end. An explicit instant expiry is. Change and Only-allow do not share one midnight range end. |
| **Evaluation** | For each candidate occurrence or instant in the **collected horizon**, decide whether this declaration applies. A horizon may contain instants before, inside, and after the lifetime in one run. |

**Selectors (meaning locked; storage spelling Open engineering).** The last selected occurrence date and an explicit instant expiry are different. A universal midnight after the last selected date is not the lifetime end for both Change and Only-allow.

- **One-date** — one schedule-TZ civil date. It selects that occurrence. It is not an instant expiry. Lifetime is that occurrence, including its overnight tail when the op is a replacement or addition of that occurrence. Later matches of the same weekday are not included. SPEC §4.4 “one specified Monday.”
- **Weekday-standing** — a mask of schedule-TZ weekdays. It matches the occurrence’s **start** day. “Every Monday” is not “this Monday.” With no expiry, it persists until targeted removal. With an explicit instant expiry, the mask applies only inside that lifetime.
- **Date-range** — inclusive schedule-TZ civil **start dates**, first through last. The last date is the last **selected occurrence date**, not an explicit instant expiry. Do not assume every civil day is 24 elapsed hours. Any civil midnight a clock does use is resolved with the approved DST rules.
- **Change range** — selects those start dates inclusively and includes each selected occurrence **in full**, overnight tail included. Monday–Monday, replacement Monday 22:00–Tuesday 02:00, **no expiry**: the Tuesday tail stays through 02:00. The range end does not clip at Tuesday 00:00.
- **Only-allow instant lifetime** — defined independently of the Change range. It is not that full-occurrence end, and not a midnight range end shared with Change. The selector names the allowed days or dates. Inside the lifetime, eligibility is by instant: a Monday-only rule excludes the Tuesday portion of an overnight window while the lifetime covers that Tuesday instant. Absent an explicit instant expiry, a date-range Only-allow runs from the start of the first selected civil date until the start of the civil day after the last selected date. That bound ends **Only-allow only**. It does not clip a Change tail, and Change’s full-occurrence inclusion does not keep Only-allow open through an overnight tail. Weekday-standing Only-allow with no expiry still persists until removal and keeps forbidding unselected instants while that lifetime covers them.
- **Optional explicit instant expiry** — a separate exclusive end instant. It is the only declared end that **clips a tail**. Monday–Monday plus an explicit Tuesday 01:00 expiry cuts that overnight replacement at 01:00. Preview shows the cut. A civil selector plus one explicit instant expiry is a combined declaration: **validate** the resulting lifetime; do not silently change the civil labels or the fixed instant. Two contradictory **instant** ends **reject, no write**. Absent expiry on a weekday-standing rule means “until removal.” Absent expiry on a Change date-range means the selected occurrences in full.

**Change vs Only-allow while the lifetime covers the candidate:**

- **Change selected days** participates only on selected occurrences. Unselected days do not get this rule; inheritance continues. Replacing Monday 22:00–Tuesday 02:00 replaces the **Tuesday tail too**. A Monday–Monday Change range with no expiry keeps that tail. The last selected date does not clip it at local midnight.
- **Only-allow Monday** forbids Tuesday **while Only-allow’s own lifetime still covers Tuesday**. It does not disappear on Tuesday the way an unselected Change day disappears, and it does not inherit Change’s full-occurrence tail. Outside that lifetime, Tuesday resumes the underlying arrangement. Only-allow is actual instants: a Monday overnight tail that falls on Tuesday is not eligible merely because the occurrence started Monday, while the exclusive rule’s lifetime covers that Tuesday instant. A separate Monday-only restriction excludes the Tuesday portion (approved overnight rule).
- Only a **separately explicit instant expiry** may cut an occurrence, and it cuts at **that** instant. Preview shows the cut. A date-range end does not. An accidental cut at local midnight, because the civil date rolled, is forbidden.

**Schedule timezone vs stored zone (civil labels vs fixed instants):**

- **Civil labels** — recurring dates, weekday masks, date selectors, and recurring window wall clocks — are labels in the **configured schedule timezone**. They do not carry a per-row stored IANA zone. A later schedule-timezone change re-reads those labels in the new schedule timezone. It does not rebase them through the old zone’s UTC bounds. The change is a settings write and must stale affected previews (timezone is already a fingerprint input). Wall-clock definitions on these recurring labels are preserved across **DST** transitions; that is not the same event as changing the IANA zone.
- **Fixed instants** — explicitly resolved one-time boundaries and explicit instant expiries — **retain the chosen UTC instant** (preserve-instant; the previously approved rule). A schedule-timezone change does not reinterpret them. They display in the new timezone. The same settings write still stales affected previews.
- A declaration that combines civil selectors and fixed instants **validates** the resulting lifetime. It does not silently change either the civil labels or the fixed instant.
- **Legacy** `time_map_overrides` keep **V3-P1 (a)** stored-zone civil dates. A schedule-timezone change does not rewrite those rows or rename those civil dates. Band hours stay in the schedule timezone; the override’s stored zone defines date scope only. V4b does not merge the two clocks.

**DST.** Reuse the approved rules. Do not write a second resolver. Gap boundary → first valid instant after the gap; do not shift the whole window to preserve duration; zero-duration occurrence is skipped and explained. Recurring fold → first occurrence for start and end, with an optional explicit second-occurrence override. One-time ambiguous boundary → the user chooses first or second and the offset is shown; preserve the resolved instant. That preserved instant is a **fixed instant**: a later schedule-timezone change retains the chosen UTC instant, displays it in the new timezone, and stales the preview. It does not re-read the instant as a civil label. One-time nonexistent boundary → flag and offer a valid replacement before save. Effort counts elapsed minutes. Scheduler, effective read, and CLI agree.

#### Temporary family replace / clear / revert (locked)

These are the V4 family ops **with a required selector**. They are not the standing ops, and they are not “do the standing op, then guess a restore later.”

Standing family replace, clear, and revert (V4-P1 / V4-P2, no selector) stay as in the V4 matrix. Sending a date or weekday selector to those standing writers **rejects, no write**.

| Operation | Stub | Scope | Selector | Lifetime | Clear / remove / revert meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scoped family replace** | `assignment.family_replace_scoped` | One of task, project, epic | One-date, weekday mask, or date-range | That selector, optional expiry | Replaces the local assignment family **only** for occurrences inside the lifetime. Outside, the previous family still participates. Stores the prior local assignment so **this identified replacement** can be reverted. Not OPT-CONVERT. Not a standing replace. |
| **Scoped clear** | `assignment.clear_scoped` | Same | Selector required | That selector, optional expiry | While active, **suppress** the identified local participation and resolve inheritance for that lifetime. Does not delete the standing local assignment. Does not apply the suppression on other days. Does not restore a displaced map. On expiry or removal, **remove that suppression** and resolve the **complete current assignment stack, including the current local assignment**. If the local assignment was genuinely removed meanwhile, inheritance wins. Do not implement this as a standing clear plus a guessed restore of the old payload. Not revert. |
| **Scoped revert** | `assignment.revert_scoped` | Same scope as the identified scoped replacement | The replacement’s selector | Restores only the captured prior for that replacement | Revision-checked (expected revision / current assignment). Does not rewind parent settings or shared policy hours. Does not move blocks. Stale revision, or a removed/unusable prior map or policy → **reject with a repair choice**, no silent fallback. **Removal after the baseline changes.** Edits to the underlying rule while a temporary winner is active are real saves. Expiry or targeted removal of the winner then shows that **current** underlying, not a snapshot taken when the temporary rule was created. For a scoped clear, that current resolution is the complete assignment stack after the suppression lifts, including the current local assignment: local **B**, parent **A** while the clear is active, and edited local **B2** when the clear expires or is removed. If that local assignment was genuinely removed during the clear, inheritance wins. Revert is the one op that restores the captured prior, and it still does not rewind parents or shared policy hours (V4-P2).

#### Overlap and precedence (V4b-P3) — locked

No save-order winner. No shortest-range winner. No last-write winner.

| Situation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Additions, same policy, overlapping dates** | Combine. Overlapping minutes count **once**. This is not a conflict. |
| **Replacements, same policy, overlapping dates** | Explicit **which-replaces-which**, naming **stable scoped targets** (the operations’ identities, not array position or save time). |
| **Addition overlapping a replacement** | Explicit choice whether the added window **survives**. Omitting that choice when the overlap exists → **reject, no write**. Do not infer it. |
| **Different named targets** | Date overlap alone is **not** a conflict. Changing Study does not rewrite Work or General. Unrelated policies do not fight because their clocks overlap. |
| **Unresolved or cyclic precedence** | **Atomic reject.** Draft preserved. Saved configuration unchanged. Unrelated rules preserved. |
| **Winner expires or is removed** | The **currently applicable** underlying arrangement returns. Not the loser’s discarded hours, and not a guessed pre-image. |
| **Same-name siblings; replacement output clobbers a sibling** | Stay on the **V4** regression shield (`RULE_NAME_CONFLICT` / `replacement_output_clobbers_sibling`). Dated overlap does not replace that rule. |

Which-replaces-which and the addition-survives bit are required product choices on the write. The field names are **Open engineering**.

**A14c bounded counterfactual (locked baseline).** Compare the preference-selected placement with an eligible fallback under the **same** collected inputs, hard constraints, pins, chunking, and buffers. Bounds:

1. **Input bound** — one collected input set and its horizon. Not a new global search.
2. **Comparison bound** — that preference-selected placement versus the eligible fallback (cross-day alternatives inside the horizon count). Not a third invented placement, and not a claim of global optimality.
3. **Repair bound** — if the preference-selected placement fails a protection check, take the eligible fallback when the fallback passes. If neither passes, keep the hard failure and explain it. Do not run an unbounded repair loop.
4. **Exhausted bound** — if the horizon ends before remaining effort is placed, **report that the bound was exhausted**. Do not call the miss unavoidable just because the horizon ended, and do not call the schedule optimal.

**Protect completion of remaining effort**, not “one chunk landed in Prefer.” Sorting tasks (priority, then UPS, then the existing stable tie) is the order. It is **not** a sufficient guarantee: a higher-ranked task that is still dependency-blocked can lose its only feasible slot to an earlier preferred placement. The comparison has to see that.

Configuration writes stay separate from preview/apply. Saving a preference does not move blocks.

**Ranking reference.** Preserve existing order: task priority, then higher UPS, then the scheduler’s existing stable tie. Do not break ties by save time or by who preferred harder. UPS remains an ordering weight whose inputs are the first of `deadline_at`, `soft_target_at`, `due_at` (already the UPS clock). “Higher-ranked” means earlier in that existing order. The deadline miss being protected is `deadline_at`. Evaluation uses the collected horizon’s candidate instants, not “whatever day the job woke up.”

**Unmet preference** names the decision that actually placed outside Prefer: deadline fallback, higher-ranked competition, or hard infeasibility. A generic outside-window flag is not that explanation. Reason-code spelling is **Open engineering**.

**Required A14c examples (Dex §13 — locked acceptance homes; fixtures = Implementation + Quality):**

1. Prefer fits one chunk and then remaining effort misses the deadline, while an earlier eligible fallback finishes. The fallback wins. The miss was avoidable.
2. A higher-ranked dependent task’s only slot is taken because its prerequisite, or an earlier lower-ranked placement, uses a preferred later window. The feasible fallback keeps that slot. Cross-day, not only later the same day.
3. Both choices meet the deadlines and preserve higher-ranked work. Prefer wins.
4. Hard requirements or pins make the work infeasible either way. They stay untouched. The failure is explained. Prefer does not weaken them.

## 5. Contract (authority + Trish lock — not open invent)

SPEC §4.2–§4.4, the approved overnight/DST rules, cutover **A14b** / **A14c**, and Dex §13 baselines **as Trish locked them (2026-10-14)**. The executable rows are §2.1. **Day-scoped (SPEC §4.3 + §2.1):**

1. **Change selected days** and **Only-allow selected days/times** are different representations. Change replaces the targeted window on selected occurrences only, including the overnight tail of a selected start day, and does not participate on unselected days. A Change range selects start dates inclusively and includes each selected overnight occurrence in full. Only-allow is hard eligibility for its own lifetime: unselected instants stay forbidden until that lifetime ends. Only-allow’s instant lifetime is not Change’s full-occurrence end. Neither is inferred from the other.
2. Offer one specific date or a recurring weekday (and a date-range, which V4 deferred and this vertical now authorizes). One-date changes expire onto the underlying schedule. A replacement removes the original window for that scope. It does not sit beside it.
3. Omit / inherit, clear, and explicit empty stay distinct. An active empty, including empty exclusive, closes the targeted availability with no ancestor and no General fall-through. Deadline and priority do not weaken that.
4. Expiry or targeted removal restores the **currently applicable** underlying arrangement and previews that consequence. A scoped clear, while active, suppresses the identified local participation and resolves inheritance. On expiry or removal, that suppression is removed and the complete current assignment stack is resolved, **including the current local assignment**. An edit to that local assignment during the clear is what returns (B → A while active → B2 on expiry). If the local assignment was genuinely removed meanwhile, inheritance wins. A temporary family clear is not a standing clear followed by a guessed restore. Scoped revert restores only the captured prior of an identified replacement (V4-P2) and is not how expiry is implemented.

**Clocks (Dex V4b-P2, R1 and R3 text reconciliation):** occurrence, lifetime, and evaluation are the three clocks in §2.1. The last selected occurrence date is not an explicit instant expiry. A Change range selects start dates inclusively and includes the full selected overnight occurrence. Only a separately explicit instant expiry clips a tail. Only-allow’s instant lifetime is defined independently and is not a universal midnight range end shared with Change. Resolve each candidate occurrence or instant across the collected horizon. Do not clip at midnight because the civil date rolled. Recurring civil labels and date selectors use the schedule timezone and are re-read if that timezone changes. Explicitly resolved one-time boundaries and explicit instant expiries retain the chosen UTC instant and display in the new timezone (preserve-instant). A declaration that combines both validates the resulting lifetime and does not silently change either. Legacy overrides keep the stored-zone contract. Approved DST rules are reused, including an explicit choice for a one-time ambiguous boundary; that choice is the preserved instant.

**Dated exceptions / overlap (SPEC §4.4 + Dex V4b-P3):**

1. Exceptions target a rule and a scope: add, replace named, or waive named. **No** ignore-all. Context stays **V7**.
2. Resolve authorized scoped exceptions into effective inputs **before** the May/Must/Prefer stack.
3. Additions to the same policy combine; overlapping minutes count once.
4. Replacements of the same policy and overlapping dates need explicit which-replaces-which on stable scoped targets. Addition-over-replacement needs an explicit survive-or-not choice. Unresolved or cyclic precedence rejects atomically; the draft is kept; saved configuration and unrelated rules stay.
5. Different named targets do not conflict merely because their dates overlap. Removal or expiry of the winner restores the currently applicable underlying. No last-write and no shortest-range winner.
6. Same-name siblings and replacement-output collisions remain the V4 regression shield.

**Ranking protection (SPEC §4.2 / A14c / Dex V4b-P4):**

1. Preserve existing order: priority, then higher UPS, then the existing stable tie. Preferences choose among eligible placements. They do not create availability or waive a requirement.
2. Avoidable means: remaining effort misses `deadline_at` under the preference-selected placement, and the eligible fallback under the same collected inputs, hard constraints, pins, chunking, and buffers completes that remaining effort. One chunk inside Prefer is not success.
3. When both placements meet the deadlines and preserve higher-ranked work, Prefer wins. Higher-ranked includes a dependency-delayed task whose only feasible slot the preferred placement would consume. Task sort order alone does not prove that.
4. The search is the named bounds in §3 (one collected set, preference vs eligible fallback, one repair step, report when the horizon is exhausted). No global optimum.
5. Hard requirements and pins stay protected. Explain unmet preference from the deadline or competition decision that actually fired.
6. Configuration apply and schedule preview/apply stay separate transactions (SPEC §7). Saving config stales previews and does not move blocks.
